TEMPLATE #1 — MARKET RESEARCH

USE THIS before you build anything.

PROMPT:
"Research the market for: [DESCRIBE BUSINESS IDEA]

Analyze:

1) Target market size (TAM - total addressable market)
2) Customer segments (who exactly needs this)
3) Customer pain points (problems they face)
4) Existing solutions (what are they using now)
5) Competitive landscape (who else plays here)
6) Market trends (growing or declining)
7) Barriers to entry (what makes it hard)
8) Go-to-market channels (how to reach customers)

Business idea: [E.g., AI-powered personal trainer app]
Geographic focus: [US / Europe / Global]

Provide:
- Market size estimate (TAM, SAM, SOM)
- Customer segment analysis
- Competitive comparison
- Trend analysis
- Go-to-market recommendations"

ARVEN TIP: Big market ≠ easy money. Find underserved segments.

TEMPLATE #2 — VALUE PROPOSITION

USE THIS to articulate why customers need you.

PROMPT:
"Define value proposition for: [DESCRIBE PRODUCT/SERVICE]

Answer:

1) Customer problem (what pain does it solve)
2) Your solution (how you solve it)
3) Why you (why you vs competitors)
4) Key benefits (not features - outcomes)
5) Target customer (who benefits most)
6) Pricing promise (cost vs value)
7) One-liner pitch (15 seconds, no jargon)

Product: [E.g., Project management for remote teams]
Customer: [E.g., Distributed startups]

Provide:
- Full value proposition statement
- Positioning statement (for each segment)
- Elevator pitch (3 versions: 10s, 30s, 60s)
- Feature-to-benefit mapping"

ARVEN TIP: If you can't explain it in one sentence, it's not clear enough.

TEMPLATE #3 — BUSINESS MODEL CANVAS

USE THIS to map your entire business.

PROMPT:
"Complete Business Model Canvas for: [DESCRIBE BUSINESS]

Define:

1) Customer segments (who are your customers)
2) Value proposition (what value you deliver)
3) Channels (how you reach customers)
4) Customer relationships (how you support them)
5) Revenue streams (how you make money)
6) Key resources (what you need to operate)
7) Key activities (what you do daily)
8) Key partnerships (who helps you)
9) Cost structure (what costs you money)

Business: [E.g., Subscription SaaS for HR]

Provide:
- Completed canvas
- Financial summary (revenue model)
- Resource requirements
- Key metrics (unit economics, CAC, LTV)
- Assumptions to validate"

ARVEN TIP: Your business model is a hypothesis. Test every box.

TEMPLATE #4 — FINANCIAL PROJECTIONS

USE THIS to show investors the upside.

PROMPT:
"Build 3-year financial projections for: [DESCRIBE BUSINESS]

Project:

1) Revenue (conservative, base, optimistic cases)
2) Customer acquisition (monthly growth)
3) Costs (COGS, operating expenses, R&D)
4) Profitability path (when you break even)
5) Cash flow (monthly for 12 months, yearly after)
6) Unit economics (CAC, LTV, payback period)
7) Key assumptions (growth rates, pricing, etc.)
8) Sensitivity (what changes break the model)

Business model: [Subscription / Marketplace / Licensing / Freemium]
Target revenue (year 1): [$X million]
Target customers (year 3): [N customers]

Provide:
- 3-year P&L statement
- Cash flow forecast
- Break-even analysis
- Unit economics
- Key assumptions list
- Sensitivity analysis"

ARVEN TIP: Be realistic. Investors know startup failure rates.

TEMPLATE #5 — PITCH DECK STRUCTURE

USE THIS to raise money or sign customers.

PROMPT:
"Structure pitch deck for: [DESCRIBE BUSINESS]

Create slides:

1) Hook (grab attention, the big problem)
2) Problem (why it matters, market size)
3) Solution (your unique approach)
4) Market opportunity (TAM and why now)
5) Business model (how you make money)
6) Traction (proof it works - customer, revenue, growth)
7) Team (why you can execute)
8) Financials (path to profitability)
9) Ask (funding or partnership request)
10) Close (memorable ending)

Audience: [Investors / Customers / Partners]
Raise: [$X million]

Provide:
- Slide outline with talking points
- Data and metrics for each slide
- Design recommendations
- Storytelling flow
- Objection responses"

ARVEN TIP: Show traction. Prove your idea works before asking for money.

TEMPLATE #6 — GO-TO-MARKET STRATEGY

USE THIS to launch and acquire customers.

PROMPT:
"Design go-to-market strategy for: [DESCRIBE PRODUCT]

Plan:

1) Target customer (who you're selling to first)
2) Distribution channels (how to reach them)
3) Sales model (direct / self-serve / inside sales)
4) Pricing strategy (how much to charge)
5) Marketing tactics (awareness and demand generation)
6) Customer acquisition (CAC, payback period)
7) Retention strategy (keep them happy)
8) Success metrics (KPIs to track)
9) Timeline (launch to scale)

Product: [E.g., B2B SaaS for HR automation]
Initial market: [SMBs in US]
Launch timeline: [3 months]

Provide:
- Go-to-market plan (month by month)
- Channel strategy and budget
- Pricing recommendation with justification
- Marketing calendar
- CAC and LTV targets
- Launch checklist"

ARVEN TIP: Distribution > Product. Great products fail with bad GTM.

TEMPLATE #7 — KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS

USE THIS to track what matters.

PROMPT:
"Define KPIs for: [DESCRIBE BUSINESS]

Track:

1) Customer acquisition (CAC, CAC payback, conversion rate)
2) Customer retention (churn rate, LTV, repeat purchase)
3) Revenue (MRR, ARR, ARPU, gross margin)
4) Operational (burn rate, runway, burn multiple)
5) Product (usage metrics, engagement, feature adoption)
6) Market (market share, NPS, customer satisfaction)
7) Team (hiring, retention, productivity)

Business model: [SaaS / Marketplace / DTC / B2B]
Stage: [Pre-launch / Early traction / Growth / Scale]

Provide:
- KPI dashboard (what to track)
- Target ranges (by stage)
- Benchmarks (vs industry)
- Reporting cadence
- Early warning signals (red flags)
- Win conditions (success metrics)"

ARVEN TIP: Pick 5-7 KPIs max. Too many = noise.
